Discovery Call Script & 5 Follow-up Emails

A 30-minute discovery call that serves the client first — and five follow-up emails for every outcome.

By Coach David Adeoye Abodunrin
AI & Automation Coach · Master Futurist · Founder, DeoCoach

The 30-minute discovery call

MinutesStageWhat to say or ask
0–3Welcome"Thank you for making time. This call is about you: I'll ask some questions, you'll get some clarity, and at the end we'll see together whether working with me makes sense. Is that okay?"
3–10Where are you now?"What made you reach out now?" · "What's working? What isn't?" · "What have you already tried?"
10–17Where do you want to be?"Imagine it's six months from now and this has gone really well. What's different?" · "How would you know?" · "Why does that matter to you?"
17–22What's in the way?"What's stopped you getting there so far?" · "What happens if nothing changes?"
22–27How I can helpReflect back their goal in their words. Describe how you work in three sentences. Share one relevant example (with permission, no names).
27–30Next step"Based on what you've shared, I think I can help. Would you like me to send a short proposal with options?" — then stop talking.
Golden rule: the client should talk for at least 70% of the call. Take notes in their exact words — you'll use them in the proposal.

Five follow-up emails

1. Same day — the proposal

Subject: Your next six months — options as promised Hi [Name], Thank you for today. I heard that you want [their goal, in their words], and that [obstacle] has been getting in the way. As promised, here are three ways we could work together: [link or attachment]. If one feels right, reply with its name and I'll send the booking link. If you'd like to talk it through, I'm happy to. Warmly, [Your name]

2. Day 3 — the gentle nudge

Subject: A quick thought on [their goal] Hi [Name], I was thinking about what you said: "[their words]". Here's one small thing you could try this week, whatever you decide: [one practical tip]. The options I sent are still open. Any questions, just reply. [Your name]

3. "I need to think about it"

Subject: Taking time is wise Hi [Name], taking time to decide is wise. To help, here are the two questions most people find useful: what would change if you achieved [goal] in the next six months, and what will it cost you if nothing changes? I'll hold your preferred start date until [date]. After that I'll open it to others. [Your name]

4. "It's too expensive"

Subject: Options that fit Hi [Name], thank you for being honest about the budget. I'd rather you start well than stretch too far. Two options: we could begin with the Essential package and upgrade later, or spread the investment over [3] monthly payments. Would either of those work? [Your name]

5. Day 14 — the respectful close

Subject: Closing the loop Hi [Name], I haven't heard back, so I'll assume now isn't the right time — that's completely fine. I'll keep you on my list for occasional insights. Whenever you're ready, reply "ready" and we'll pick up where we left off. Wishing you every success with [their goal]. [Your name]
